# Crossings — grounded sleep mask

Chosen before drawing: a padded travel sleep mask laid face up in the 30° isometric plane. A broad elastic band is folded behind and partly hidden by the mask. The mask rests on the same surface as its band; shallow thickness and a stitched edge give it weight without a prop.

This connects clock shifts with disrupted sleep through an everyday travel object. The figure does not measure lost sleep or claim melatonin use. The existing 82 h, 39 forward / 43 back, and 7 of 24 hourly offsets remain in the sheet text and charts.

One neutral object, two restrained cushion creases, one accent piping segment. No bottle, tiny wording, new data display, particles or glow. The full-sheet study duplicates the approved stages, modifying only its figure.

Checkpoints: spacing has clear space around the mask; typography is unchanged; neutral contours and one coral seam remain visible; artwork is optically centered; no clipping at figure or sheet edge; eye cups share material but the nose notch breaks geometric repetition.

Rejected: bottle and tiny label (visual noise and medical implication); numerical display or pills as metrics (unsupported data); glow (fabric cannot emit light); automatic motion (unneeded attention).

Review boundary: the static coral textile piping is a deliberate exception to the current illustration rule that accent belongs to a glowing live part. No sleep object has a truthful illuminated part. Parent must assess that semantic choice. The figure is a sleep/jetlag metaphor; it does not encode the measured clock shift. No interaction states or app behavior were changed or verified.

Verdict: ready for parent design review; no fit, spacing or silhouette issue remains in the inspected static artwork. The accent convention is the only open design tradeoff.
